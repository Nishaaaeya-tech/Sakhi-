Common concerns, explained

Short guides on what is common, what may help, and when to see a gynecologist.

PCOD / PCOS

Polycystic ovarian disease or syndrome is a hormonal condition. Ovaries may release eggs irregularly, and androgen (male hormone) levels can be higher.

Common signs:

  • Irregular or missed periods
  • Acne, oily skin, excess facial or body hair
  • Scalp hair thinning
  • Weight gain, especially around the belly
  • Trouble getting pregnant

What helps: regular movement, a balanced low-glycaemic diet, good sleep, stress care, and medical treatment when needed. Even a 5 to 10% weight loss can help restore regular cycles in some women who are overweight.

Diagnosis needs a doctor, usually with an ultrasound and blood tests.

Late or missed periods

A cycle of 21 to 35 days is considered normal for adults. Stress, travel, illness, sudden weight change, thyroid problems, PCOS and pregnancy can all delay a period.

  • If sexually active, take a pregnancy test first.
  • Note stress, sleep and weight changes.
  • Missing 3 or more periods in a row needs a doctor’s visit.
Painful or heavy periods

Mild cramps are common. Pain that stops you from daily life is not something you must put up with. Heavy bleeding means soaking a pad or tampon every hour for several hours, or passing large clots.

  • Heat packs, light walking and gentle yoga can ease cramps.
  • Possible causes include fibroids, endometriosis, adenomyosis, thyroid or clotting issues.
  • Heavy bleeding can cause anaemia: tiredness, dizziness and pale skin are warning signs.
Thyroid and your cycle

An underactive or overactive thyroid can change cycle length and flow. A simple TSH blood test can check it. Ask your doctor if you also notice fatigue, weight change, hair loss or feeling too cold or hot.

See a doctor soon if: bleeding is very heavy, pain is severe, you have a fever with pelvic pain, you bleed after menopause or between periods, or you have had no period for 3 months or more.

Weight and BMI check

Simple habits that help

  • Walk 30 minutes a day, and add strength work 2 to 3 times a week.
  • Fill half your plate with vegetables, a quarter with protein, a quarter with whole grains.
  • Choose fruit over juice and whole grains over maida.
  • Sleep 7 to 8 hours. Poor sleep raises cravings and insulin resistance.
  • Aim for steady, slow loss. Crash diets can worsen cycle problems.

BMI cut-offs use the Asian-Indian ranges. BMI is a screening number, not a diagnosis.

Healthy 2-minute recipes

Quick snacks and breakfasts for busy days. No long cooking needed.

Banana-flax curd bowl

2 minutes, no cooking

You need: 1 cup curd, ½ banana sliced, 1 tsp roasted flaxseed powder, a pinch of cinnamon.

Mix everything in a bowl. Good for gut health, with omega-3 from the flax.

Quick sprouts chaat

2 minutes, no cooking

You need: ½ cup ready sprouts, chopped onion, tomato, lemon juice, chaat masala.

Toss and eat. High in protein and fibre, which helps steady blood sugar in PCOD.

Spinach-banana smoothie

2 minutes, blender

You need: a handful of spinach, 1 banana, 1 cup milk or plant milk, 1 tsp chia seeds.

Blend until smooth. A gentle iron and magnesium boost during your period.

Masala makhana

2 minutes, pan

You need: 1 cup makhana, 1 tsp ghee, salt, black pepper, a pinch of turmeric.

Roast in a pan on medium heat for 2 minutes until crisp. A light, low-glycaemic snack.

Cucumber-mint chaas

2 minutes, blender

You need: 1 cup curd, ½ cup water, ½ cucumber, mint leaves, roasted jeera powder.

Blend and drink chilled or at room temperature. Can help with bloating before your period.

Date and nut bites

2 minutes, no cooking

You need: 2 dates (seeds removed), 4 almonds, 2 walnut halves.

Eat together or press into a small ball. Gives iron and energy on period days. If you manage blood sugar, keep to 2 dates.

Skin and hair care

Hormones, stress, food and sleep show up on your skin and hair. Many women with irregular periods or PCOD notice acne, dullness or hair fall too, so we cover them together.

Common skin and hair concerns

Acne and period breakouts

Pimples often appear along the chin and jaw a week before your period, when hormones shift. PCOD can also cause stubborn acne because of higher androgen levels.

  • Wash your face twice a day with a gentle cleanser.
  • Use products with salicylic acid or niacinamide, starting a few times a week.
  • Do not pick or squeeze, which causes marks and scars.
  • Limit sugary foods and sweet drinks, which may worsen acne for some women.

See a dermatologist if acne is deep, painful, scarring or does not improve in 8 to 12 weeks.

Dark spots and uneven skin tone

Sun, old pimples, hormones and friction can leave dark patches. Dark, velvety patches on the neck, underarms or groin can be a sign of insulin resistance, which is common in PCOD, so mention them to your doctor.

  • Apply sunscreen every day. It is the most important step.
  • Vitamin C serum in the morning and niacinamide can help fade marks over months.
Dry, dull or oily skin

Dry skin needs a richer moisturiser and fewer hot showers. Oily skin still needs a light, gel-based moisturiser. Dullness often improves with water, sleep, protein and gentle exfoliation once or twice a week.

Unwanted facial or body hair

Extra dark hair on the chin, upper lip, chest or belly can come from higher androgen levels, often linked to PCOD. Hair removal methods such as threading, waxing or laser manage the hair, but they do not treat the cause.

Ask a doctor for a hormone check, especially if hair growth is fast or comes with irregular periods.

Hair fall and thinning

Losing 50 to 100 hairs a day is normal. More than that, a wider parting, or a thinner ponytail needs attention. Common causes are low iron, low vitamin D or B12, thyroid problems, PCOD, crash diets, stress, illness and childbirth.

  • Eat enough protein and iron-rich foods.
  • Be gentle: avoid tight hairstyles and very hot styling.
  • Get a blood test for iron (ferritin), vitamin D, B12 and thyroid (TSH).
Dandruff and itchy scalp

Flakes and itching usually come from a yeast-like fungus on an oily scalp, not from dryness. Wash with an anti-dandruff shampoo (ketoconazole, zinc pyrithione or selenium sulfide) two to three times a week, leaving it on for 3 minutes before rinsing.

See a doctor if: hair falls in patches, you lose a lot of hair suddenly, acne is severe, or skin and hair changes come with missed periods, fatigue or fast weight change.

Skin care tips and tricks

Keep it simple

Morning: cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen SPF 30 or higher. Night: cleanser, moisturiser, and one treatment (such as retinol or a pimple gel) if needed.

Sunscreen every day

Use about two finger-lengths for face and neck, even indoors and on cloudy days. Reapply every 2 to 3 hours if you are outside.

Patch test first

Try any new product on your inner arm for 24 hours. Add only one new product at a time so you know what suits you.

Clean pillowcase and phone

Change pillowcases weekly and wipe your phone screen often. Both touch your skin for hours.

Sleep and water

Aim for 7 to 8 hours of sleep and regular water. Skin repairs itself at night.

Match your cycle

Use gentler products in the week before your period, when skin is more sensitive and breakout-prone. Skin is usually calmest after your period ends.

Haircare routine

Every dayGently detangle with a wide-tooth comb, starting from the ends. Keep hair loosely tied, and sleep with a loose braid or a silk scrunchie.
2 to 3 times a weekShampoo your scalp and condition only the lengths. Use lukewarm water, and finish with a cool rinse.
Once a weekWarm oil massage for 5 to 10 minutes, left on for 1 to 2 hours before shampooing. Or use a hair mask.
Every monthTrim split ends (about 1 cm) every 8 to 12 weeks to stop breakage travelling up the hair.
AvoidVery hot water, daily heat styling, tight ponytails, and combing hair when it is soaking wet.

Hair fall care recipes

Home care can improve scalp health and reduce breakage. It cannot fix hair fall caused by low iron, thyroid or hormone problems, so get those checked too. Do a patch test before using any mix, and avoid broken or irritated skin.

Curry leaf coconut oil

Weekly oil

You need: 1 cup coconut oil, a handful of fresh curry leaves.

Warm the oil on low heat, add the leaves and heat until they crackle. Cool, strain and store. Massage into the scalp, leave 1 hour, then shampoo.

Methi curd mask

Weekly mask

You need: 2 tbsp fenugreek seeds soaked overnight, 3 tbsp curd.

Grind into a paste and mix with curd. Apply to scalp and hair for 30 minutes, then rinse with a mild shampoo.

Aloe vera scalp gel

Soothing treatment

You need: 3 tbsp fresh aloe vera gel, 1 tsp coconut oil.

Blend and massage into a clean scalp. Leave for 30 minutes, then rinse. Helpful for itch and dryness.

Egg and curd protein mask

Monthly boost

You need: 1 egg, 2 tbsp curd, 1 tsp olive oil.

Whisk and apply for 20 minutes. Rinse with cool water, never hot, so the egg does not cook. Skip if you are vegetarian or allergic.

Amla and hibiscus rinse

Wash-day rinse

You need: 1 tbsp amla powder, 4 hibiscus flowers, 2 cups water.

Boil for 5 minutes, cool and strain. Pour over clean hair as a final rinse and leave for 5 minutes before rinsing out.

Hair-strength laddoo

Eat 1 a day

You need: 1 cup roasted sesame, ½ cup flaxseed powder, ½ cup chopped dates, 2 tbsp ghee.

Mix and roll into small balls. Provides iron, zinc and healthy fats. If you manage blood sugar, keep portions small.

In short: clear skin and strong hair start from the inside. Eat enough protein and iron, sleep well, manage stress and move your body. Keep skin and hair routines simple and gentle, use sunscreen daily, and treat recipes as support, not a cure. If skin or hair changes come with irregular periods, check hormones, iron, vitamin D and thyroid with your doctor.
